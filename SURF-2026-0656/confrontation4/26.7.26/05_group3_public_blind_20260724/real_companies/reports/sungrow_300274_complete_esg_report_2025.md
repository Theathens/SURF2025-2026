# Sungrow Power Supply Co., Ltd. (阳光电源股份有限公司) ESG Report 2025

## Report status and scope

This document is an evidence-grounded reconstruction of a complete ESG report using the available 2022–2025 public-company disclosure set. It is not represented as an official republication of the company's original report. Quantitative values retain their disclosed year, unit and boundary; unavailable indicators remain undisclosed rather than being estimated or replaced with zero.

The structured evidence does not provide equal coverage for every company-year or ESG topic. Comparisons should therefore consider reporting boundary, location-based versus market-based electricity accounting, Scope 3 category coverage, unit definitions and whether values were disclosed directly or derived by transparent normalization.

## Company and sustainability context

Sungrow Power Supply Co., Ltd. operates in photovoltaic inverter, energy-storage and clean-energy equipment manufacturing. Sungrow's material topics include operational and value-chain emissions, renewable electricity, product carbon performance, energy efficiency, responsible supply chains, occupational safety, product quality and innovation for clean-energy systems.

Sustainability is managed through the interaction of strategy, operations, product responsibility, people management, supply-chain standards and compliance. Environmental performance must be read together with output, geographic footprint and reporting-boundary changes; favorable intensity movement alone does not establish an absolute reduction.

## 2025 evidence highlights

- The 2024 public disclosure reports Scope 1 emissions of 6,426 tCO2e, Scope 2 emissions of 40,061 tCO2e and Scope 3 emissions of 9,062,835 tCO2e.
- Renewable electricity represented 70% in 2024 and 79% in 2025.
- The 2025 performance table reports total electricity consumption of 286,544.79 MWh, direct green-electricity purchases of 149,875.31 MWh and onsite generation of 14,634.8937 MWh.
- Public targets cover operational Scope 1+2 carbon neutrality by 2028, supply-chain carbon neutrality by 2038 and supply-chain net zero by 2048.

## ESG governance

Board and management oversight of sustainability should connect material ESG matters to enterprise risk, capital allocation, operational control and disclosure quality. Responsible functions include environmental management, health and safety, human resources, procurement, quality, product stewardship, information security, finance and compliance.

The available metric set does not consistently disclose ESG committee meeting counts, attendance, incentive linkage or environmental-training denominators. This report therefore does not invent governance coverage percentages. Future disclosure should identify the accountable body, decision frequency, escalation threshold and source owner for each material indicator.

### Risk management and data control

Material environmental risks include energy-price exposure, transition policy, renewable-electricity availability, climate-related physical disruption, water stress, waste and pollution compliance. Social risks include occupational safety, workforce capability, labor rights and supplier conditions. Governance risks include bribery, conflicts, data misstatement, cybersecurity and weak evidence retention.

Reported information should pass period, unit, boundary and reconciliation checks. Original source values, normalized fields and narrative statements must remain traceable so that corrections do not silently change the historical baseline.

### Climate and emissions

Scope 1, Scope 2 and Scope 3 figures should be interpreted using the boundary stated in the underlying source. Market-based and location-based Scope 2 values are not interchangeable. Where a report discloses Scope 3 only for a particular geography or category set, it is not relabeled as a complete global value-chain total.

Climate targets are forward-looking commitments and must not be rewritten as achieved carbon-neutral status. Carbon-credit quantities, registry identifiers and retirement evidence are separate from gross emissions. Where registry-level information is not disclosed, the report does not infer it.

### Water, waste, pollution and biodiversity

The available evidence is uneven across water withdrawal, wastewater, hazardous and non-hazardous waste, pollutant emissions and biodiversity. Management approaches may include legal compliance, source reduction, segregation, responsible disposal and site assessment, but the absence of a public quantity cannot support a zero-impact or zero-incident statement.

## Social responsibility

### Employees and labor rights

A complete social-management framework should cover lawful employment, non-discrimination, freedom of association where applicable, prohibition of forced and child labor, working hours, compensation, privacy and accessible grievance mechanisms. The common dataset does not provide a complete workforce series, so headcount, turnover, diversity and grievance statistics are not invented.

### Occupational health and safety

Health and safety management should include hazard identification, engineering controls, contractor safety, training, incident investigation and emergency preparedness. Unless the source provides recordable rates, lost-time frequency and fatalities with a defined workforce boundary, the report does not state zero incidents.

### Learning and development

Training supports technical competence, safety, environmental responsibility, quality and ethical conduct. Participant counts, eligible populations and coverage percentages require both numerator and denominator; incomplete public evidence is retained as missing.

### Responsible supply chain

Supplier management should address labor, health and safety, environmental compliance, emissions, responsible materials, business ethics and information security. Reported due-diligence or audit coverage must identify the supplier population and assessment scope.

### Product responsibility and customers

Product responsibility covers electrical safety, reliability, product carbon information, responsible sourcing and service across inverter and energy-storage solutions.

Quality and customer controls should include design verification, supplier quality, manufacturing inspection, complaint handling, corrective action and transparent product information. Product claims must be distinguished from avoided-emissions estimates produced during product use.

### Communities

Community engagement should consider local employment, environmental impact, emergency response and responsible investment. The evidence set does not support a complete charitable-contribution or volunteering series, so no total is asserted.

## Responsible governance

### Business ethics

The governance framework should prohibit bribery, fraud, retaliation, conflicts of interest and anti-competitive conduct, and should provide confidential reporting and investigation processes. Training and substantiated-case counts are disclosed only where supported by public evidence.

### Data protection and cybersecurity

Data protection should follow legitimate purpose, access control, security and incident-response principles. The reconstructed dataset does not contain a complete cybersecurity event series and therefore makes no zero-event claim.

## Forward priorities

1. Improve company-year continuity for core energy, emissions, water, waste, workforce and safety indicators.
2. Clarify market-based and location-based Scope 2 methods and instrument treatment.
3. Expand Scope 3 category coverage while avoiding comparisons between incompatible boundaries.
4. Link renewable-electricity claims to procurement, certificate and meter evidence.
5. Process facility satellite variables through a documented workflow before using them as corroborating evidence.

## Data limitations

This report is complete as a structured ESG narrative, but the underlying public evidence remains partial. Missing values are preserved explicitly. The document must not be used to imply that undisclosed metrics were measured, assured or equal to zero.
