# Nova Home Systems Co., Ltd. ESG Report 2025

## About this report

This report presents the company’s environmental, social and governance approach and performance for 2022–2025. Unless otherwise stated, quantitative performance covers the reporting years ending 31 December.

The organizational boundary is **consolidated operating entities**. The operational emissions boundary is **Scope 1 direct fuel; Scope 2 purchased electricity; selected logistics and purchased-goods Scope 3**. The quantitative data package does not provide employee counts, water volumes, waste tonnage, incident rates, governance-meeting counts or assurance conclusions. Those items are therefore discussed as management approaches and are explicitly marked as not quantitatively disclosed.

The report follows four principles: materiality, consistency, balance and traceability. Numeric claims in the environmental sections reconcile to the accompanying annual ESG, green-power/carbon and facility-proxy tables. Blank values in those tables mean unavailable or not disclosed; they do not mean zero.

## Message from management

During 2025, Nova Home Systems Co., Ltd. continued to integrate responsible operations into its core business of household appliance and smart-home systems manufacturing. We view environmental performance, employee wellbeing, product responsibility and ethical conduct as operating disciplines rather than stand-alone communications topics.

The transition to a lower-carbon manufacturing model requires more than a headline target. It requires consistent energy and emissions boundaries, traceable renewable-electricity instruments, credible treatment of carbon credits, reliable facility information and transparent acknowledgment of missing data. This report therefore presents the full four-year baseline, including indicators that moved in different directions, and does not replace unavailable information with estimates.

In the next reporting cycle, management will continue improving primary-data collection, responsibility assignment and evidence retention. Particular attention will be given to Scope 3 category coverage, facility-level energy metering, environmental and social performance indicators that are not yet quantified, and the distinction between reduction activity, renewable electricity procurement and offsetting.

## Company profile and sustainability context

The company manufactures appliance and smart-home products. Its main ESG topics include factory electricity, product energy efficiency, refrigerant and material management, worker safety, supplier conduct, product quality and end-of-life circularity.

Nova's sustainability approach follows the product life cycle, from responsible procurement and efficient manufacturing to durable products, customer safety and circular design.

The company applies the same reporting boundary across the annual structured table and the narrative report. Facility-level proxy information supports directional comparison with changes in built-up area, rooftop photovoltaic area, night-time light and surface-temperature indicators.
## ESG governance

The board retains oversight of material sustainability matters and management is responsible for implementation through relevant operational functions. Environmental responsibilities are integrated across operations, procurement, quality, human resources, health and safety, finance, compliance and information management. Business units are expected to maintain source evidence for reported indicators and escalate boundary or methodology changes before publication.

The reported dataset does not disclose the number of board or committee meetings, the number of environmental training participants or the number of ISO 14001-certified facilities. This report therefore does not state quantitative governance or training coverage. Future disclosures should distinguish formal oversight, operational review and data-owner sign-off.

### Risk management and internal control

Material ESG risks are considered through ordinary enterprise-risk processes. Climate and energy risks include electricity-price exposure, transition-policy changes, physical disruption, renewable-electricity availability and supplier emissions. Social risks include occupational injury, labor-rights concerns, skills shortages and product safety. Governance risks include corruption, data misstatement, weak supplier due diligence, privacy breaches and incomplete evidence trails.

Reported performance is subject to reasonableness checks, period-on-period review and cross-table reconciliation. Changes to accounting boundaries, conversion factors or instrument classifications should be documented so that trends remain interpretable.

## Environmental management

The environmental-management approach covers legal compliance, operational controls, energy and emissions, water, waste, pollution prevention and emergency preparedness. The principal manufacturing campus maintained ISO 14001 coverage during the reporting year according to the supplied narrative; however, the available dataset does not quantify the number or percentage of certified facilities, so no group-wide certification rate is claimed.

Operational teams are expected to identify significant environmental aspects, maintain control procedures, investigate deviations and retain supporting records. Capital projects should consider energy use, emissions, resource efficiency and local environmental conditions during design and approval.

### Energy and production performance

Total energy includes electricity and the reported residual direct-energy component. Purchased electricity is calculated as total electricity less onsite photovoltaic generation for model-interface purposes. Because the available data does not split direct energy into natural gas and diesel, those fuel fields remain undisclosed.

### Climate targets and carbon credits

The stated target is: **operational carbon neutrality target by 2035; residual emissions may be offset after reduction**. The stated target boundary is **Scope 1+2 operational boundary**. This is a forward-looking target, not a claim that the company was carbon neutral during 2022–2025.

Retired credits are disclosed independently from gross Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions. The retirement quantities do not support a company-wide carbon-neutral claim and are not deducted from the emissions table. Credits are intended for residual operational emissions after direct reduction efforts.

## Social responsibility

### Employees and labor rights

The company supports lawful employment, non-discrimination, respect for freedom of association where applicable, prohibition of forced and child labor, and accessible grievance mechanisms. Workforce headcount, turnover, gender composition, training hours and grievance outcomes are not disclosed in the reported dataset and are therefore not quantified here.

### Occupational health and safety

The safety-management approach includes hazard identification, operational controls, contractor management, training, incident reporting and emergency preparedness. No lost-time injury frequency, recordable-incident rate, fatality count or coverage percentage is supplied. Absence of a number must not be rewritten as zero incidents.

### Learning and development

Training supports job competence, safety, environmental responsibility, quality and ethical conduct. The dataset does not provide participant counts, eligible population or training-coverage percentages. Future reports should disclose both numerator and denominator and explain whether contractors are included.

### Responsible supply chain

Suppliers are expected to meet requirements covering legal compliance, labor rights, health and safety, environmental performance, business ethics and information protection. Risk-based review should prioritize material-intensive, energy-intensive and higher-risk categories. The current baseline does not quantify supplier audit coverage or corrective-action closure.

### Product responsibility and customers

Product stewardship emphasizes quality assurance, energy-efficient design, restricted-substance controls, repairability considerations and responsible end-of-life treatment.

Quality and safety responsibilities include controlled design changes, supplier-quality management, production inspection, complaint handling and corrective action. Product recall, defect and customer-satisfaction indicators are not included in the available dataset.

### Communities

The company seeks constructive relations with the communities around its facilities and considers local environmental impact, emergency readiness and employment. No charitable-contribution or volunteer-hour total is included because supporting records were not supplied.

## Responsible governance

### Business ethics and compliance

The company prohibits bribery, fraud, conflicts of interest, retaliation and anti-competitive conduct. Employees and relevant business partners are expected to follow applicable laws and internal standards. Compliance-training and substantiated-case counts are not quantified in this baseline.

### Data protection and cybersecurity

Information is managed according to role-based access, legitimate-use and security principles. Material incidents should be assessed, contained and reported through established escalation processes. The available data contains no cybersecurity-event statistics.

## 2026 priorities

1. Improve primary evidence for energy, renewable-electricity instruments and emissions factors.
2. Expand Scope 3 category definitions and document completeness limitations.
3. Add verified water, waste, air-pollutant, workforce, safety and training indicators.

### Data availability statement

The structured dataset intentionally preserves missing indicators. Revenue, employee count, factory count, fuel splits, water, waste, governance-meeting counts, training participation and facility certification coverage are not available in the available records. They must remain undisclosed until supported by evidence.
